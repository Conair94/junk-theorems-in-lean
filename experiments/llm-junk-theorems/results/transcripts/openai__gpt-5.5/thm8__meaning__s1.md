This Lean file formalizes a standard “product σ-algebra cannot see uncountably many coordinates at once” argument, and applies it to the type of groups.

The main theorem is:

```lean
theorem Exists_GrpCat_nonmeasurable : ¬MeasurableSet (@Exists GrpCat)
```

In plain mathematical language:

> Let `GrpCat` be the type/category of groups. Consider the powerset `Set GrpCat`, i.e. the space of all collections of groups, equipped with the σ-algebra generated by the coordinate membership maps  
> \[
> S \mapsto \mathbf{1}_{G \in S}
> \]
> for each group \(G\).  
> Then the subset of `Set GrpCat` consisting of the nonempty collections of groups is not measurable.

Equivalently:

> The set
> \[
> \{S \subseteq \mathrm{GrpCat} \mid S \neq \varnothing\}
> \]
> is not measurable in the product σ-algebra on \(\{0,1\}^{\mathrm{GrpCat}}\).

Here `@Exists GrpCat` is Lean’s predicate

```lean
fun P : GrpCat → Prop => ∃ G : GrpCat, P G
```

and since a set of groups is represented as a predicate `GrpCat → Prop`, this is exactly the collection of all nonempty subsets of `GrpCat`.

4. It proves nonmeasurability of the nonempty-subset predicate.

   The final theorem assumes for contradiction that

   \[
   X = \{S \subseteq \mathrm{GrpCat} \mid \exists G, G \in S\}

   is measurable. By the countable-support lemma, there is a countable set \(Y\) of groups such that membership in \(X\) only depends on intersection with \(Y\).

   Since `GrpCat` is uncountable, choose a group \(G \notin Y\). Then the two subsets

   \[
   \varnothing
   \quad\text{and}\quad
   \{G\}
   \]

   have the same intersection with \(Y\), namely the empty set. Therefore the countable-support lemma would imply

   \[
   \varnothing \in X \iff \{G\} \in X.
   \]

   But \(\{G\}\) is nonempty, while \(\varnothing\) is empty. Contradiction.
